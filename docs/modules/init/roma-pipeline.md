# The RoMa pipeline in `openftgs.init`

How `roma_init` meets §1.3 "Initialization" steps 1–7 and E15: init frame slots, camera pairs, dense matching, triangulation and the assembly of each init frame into init points. Steps 8 and 9, and the trainer's total cap F·B (D-74), stay in `FreeTimeGaussians.from_init_points` (`docs/modules/model/initialization.md`). The public names are Q-106's: `roma_init(dataset, cfg, *, devices, matcher)`, the `DenseMatcher` protocol, `roma_provenance`, `knn_velocities` and `triangulate_dlt`, with `RomaInitConfig` checked on construction (Q-106, Q-122).

## Order of work

1. **Init frame slots** (D-21, D-47, D-66; Q-109). Only training records take part. The slots are every k_stride-th frame index of the sequence, F counted as in Q-21. An image joins a slot when its nearest frame is that slot's and it lies within Δt_f/2 of t_k; per camera the closest image wins, then the earlier timestamp, then the lower record index. A camera with no such image sits the slot out.
2. **Camera pairs** (D-22; Q-108, Q-109). Within a slot, each camera's partner is the camera whose optical axis is angularly closest, read from each image's own camera; ties go to the center distance, then to the manifest's camera order. Each unordered pair is matched once, oriented (A, B) in the order of the manifest's `cameras` object (`SceneManifest.camera_names`).
3. **Matching** (D-58; Q-106, Q-110). The pair's two images go to a dense matcher: RoMa per `RomaInitConfig.model` by default, or the one `matcher` builds. Every match with a coordinate outside the image, or not finite, is dropped; of the rest, those whose raw certainty reaches the threshold, and is above 0, are drawn in proportion to certainty, without replacement, with the pair's own generator, on the CPU (Q-121). A match of certainty 0 has probability 0, so it is never drawn, which matters only when the threshold is 0.
4. **Triangulation** (D-22, D-56; Q-110, Q-120). `triangulate_dlt` in float64 on the shard's device, which filters nothing; then D-22's filters on every row: finite, depth positive in both views, reprojection error at most 2 px in both, and at least 1° between the rays from the two camera centers. A color is the mean of the two source pixels, each the pixel holding the match's coordinate; the timestamp is the mean raw τ of the two images. Positions are then rounded to float32, the values the init points store (Q-111).
5. **Assembly per init frame** (E15, D-23, D-24; Q-111). A slot's points are its pairs' points in canonical order: pairs by the positions of A and B in the manifest's camera order, each pair's points in draw order. The per-init-frame cap k_stride·B keeps a seeded subset in that order; velocities come from E15 against the next slot's full point set (the previous one's on the last slot, none with a single slot), with dt from the slot times in raw units; durations are s₀ = k_stride·Δt_f in raw units (k_stride for a single timestamp, D-70); the frame ids are the slot indices. The init points come back on the CPU, validated as ftgs-init requires (Q-22, Q-122).

## Seeds

Every random draw has a CPU generator of its own, seeded from `RomaInitConfig.seed` and what the draw belongs to (Q-110, Q-111), by Q-72's hashing: the first 8 bytes, little-endian, of the SHA-256 of the UTF-8 bytes of `json.dumps(parts, separators=(",", ":"))` (Q-121), where `parts` is

- `["openftgs", "roma_init", "pair", seed, k, A, B]` for the matches of a pair, A and B being camera names;
- `["openftgs", "roma_init", "slot", seed, k]` for the subset of a slot, a generator left untouched when the slot fits.

k is the slot's frame index, a multiple of k_stride. No draw depends on another's position in a stream, so the init points don't depend on the order in which pairs are matched. Changing these bytes changes every run's draws.

## Shards

`devices` names one shard per entry, each with its own dense matcher; repeating an entry puts several shards on one GPU (Q-106). The matchers are built one after the other in the caller's thread, the default RoMa ones from weights fetched once before (Q-112). The jobs are listed slot by slot, in record order within a slot, and each shard's thread takes them from a shared queue; results are keyed by (slot, A, B) and assembled in canonical order, so how the pairs are spread over shards, or listed in the manifest, changes nothing (D-54, D-56, U-33). A slot is finished once the next slot with points is triangulated, so at most three slots' full point sets are held at a time. A shard's exception reaches the caller once every shard has stopped. E15 runs on the first device of `devices`.

## Determinism and precision

For the whole call, matcher building included, `roma_init` sets the float32 matmul precision RoMa requires, turns cuDNN autotuning off and deterministic cuDNN and strict deterministic algorithms on, and restores the previous settings afterwards, after a failure too (D-56, §2.4 "Precision"; Q-112). No op of RoMa's or Tiny RoMa's forward pass raises in strict mode (torch 2.14.1, CUDA 13.0), and real RoMa gives bitwise identical init points however the pairs are sharded on one GPU or listed in the manifest, with `use_custom_corr` off (U-33, #137). RoMa keeps its own float16 autocast. Triangulation, the filters and E15 run in float64; the k-NN of E15 is exact, ties to the lower index (D-56).

## The dense-matcher port

A dense matcher takes two images as `load_image` returns them and gives a warp in RoMa's normalized coordinates with a certainty per match (Q-106); `roma_init` refuses anything but a floating-point warp [M, 4] and certainty [M] (Q-122). `matcher` is a factory: given a shard's device, it returns that shard's dense matcher. The RoMa adapter imports the vendored copy (`docs/modules/init/vendored-roma.md`) only when it is built, so `openftgs.init` imports without the `[init]` extra. It hands RoMa PIL images of round(255·x) and returns the whole two-way warp, flattened, as float32 on the CPU; `use_custom_corr` is on only when fused-local-corr's `local_corr` imports (Q-112). The Tiny RoMa adapter loads XFeat from the commit whose license was checked, with torch.hub's validation skipped, since it accepts a commit only while that commit heads a branch or tag (Q-116's fallback), then loads that commit's weights into it, their SHA-256 prefix checked by torch.hub, rather than the weights XFeat's own loader would take from the repository's `main` (Q-123), gives each shard its own copy (Tiny RoMa deletes XFeat's unused heads when it is built) and wraps the call in inference mode (Q-116); the vendored `TinyRoMa.match` already carries an inference-mode decorator, so the wrapper changes nothing there. Tests supply an analytic matcher that knows the scene's geometry, so U-19's clauses run on the CPU; the adapters themselves are tested only with the real weights, never with a stand-in for RoMa: roma_outdoor on a GPU, Tiny RoMa on the CPU and a GPU.

## Provenance

`roma_provenance(cfg)` gives the metadata `write_init_points` records under one key, `roma_init`: compact JSON with sorted keys of every `RomaInitConfig` field, `use_custom_corr` as the import check gives it, `roma_commit` from the vendored copy's VENDOR.json and, for Tiny RoMa, `xfeat_commit` (D-58; Q-107, Q-122). It reads VENDOR.json without importing the vendored copy.
